Banks already know how to audit human decisions. The open problem is auditing AI decisions — and producing evidence that holds up independently of the system that made them.

Existing AI governance produces logs — generated by the same system being audited. NOUS produces cryptographically verifiable evidence, checkable by anyone, offline, independently of the system that made the decision.

What this proves

  • the recorded decision stayed inside the declared policy
  • the policy had no coverage gap, proven before the model ran
  • every claim is Ed25519-signed and recomputable offline
  • checkable by anyone, with zero trust in the issuer

What it does not claim

  • that the model cannot make a bad recommendation
  • that the runtime faithfully recorded reality against a hostile operator
  • that the system is “safe” or legally compliant in the abstract
  • anything it cannot itself sign — it refuses the rest
In one line: the decision was logged inside its declared policy, that policy was proven gap-free before the model ran, and you can verify all of it yourself — offline, with zero trust in any party. It makes the model’s compliance auditable after the fact; it does not make the model trustworthy.
Maps to EU AI Act Annex IV §2(g) (validation procedures & metrics) and Article 12 (record-keeping). Evidence that supports an audit — NOUS does not assert legal compliance, which is the auditor’s conclusion to draw.
